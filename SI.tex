\section{Descriptor definitions}
\label{si:defs}

Each entry below gives the descriptive name of a descriptor, as in Tables~1
and~2 of the main text, its name in pydemi in parentheses, its units in square
brackets where it has units, and its definition.

\paragraph{Notation.} The cell of volume $V$ is divided into $N_v$ voxels $k$ of
volume $\Delta V=V/N_v$; $\rho_k$ is the density in e\,\AA$^{-3}$ (the
\texttt{CHGCAR} value divided by $V$), $Q_\mathrm{tot}=\sum_k\rho_k\Delta V$ and
$\langle\rho\rangle=Q_\mathrm{tot}/V$. For each voxel, $i(k)$ is the nearest
nucleus (periodic images included), $r_k$ its distance and $\hat{\mathbf u}_k$
the unit vector from that nucleus to the voxel. \rev{The core, bond and interstitial
regions follow from the covalent radii $R_i$ (Cordero et~al., Dalton Trans.\ 2832,
2008): with $s_k=\min_i r_{ik}/R_i$ over all nuclei $i$, a voxel is core if
$s_k\le a$, bond if $a<s_k\le b$ and interstitial if $s_k>b$, with $a=0.79$ and
$b=1.14$ (Section~2 of the main text).} $\nabla\rho$, $\nabla^2\rho$ and the Hessian eigenvalues
$\lambda_1\le\lambda_2\le\lambda_3$ are computed spectrally on the periodic grid.
Quantities defined in atomic units (NCI, information measures) are evaluated in
bohr. A descriptor that is undefined for a special input returns a documented
constant and sets a flag.

\subsection{Bonding (18)}
\begin{description}\sloppy
\item[Mean charge distance, mean squared charge distance, charge-distance variance] (\dn{m1}, \dn{m2}, \dn{sigma_r2}) [\AA, \AA$^2$, \AA$^2$]
$m_n=\sum_k\rho_k r_k^n/\sum_k\rho_k$, $\sigma_r^2=m_2-m_1^2$: mean distance of
the charge from its nearest nucleus, second moment and radial spread.
\item[Core, \rev{bond-region} and interstitial charge fractions] (\dn{f_core}, \dn{f_bond}, \dn{f_int}) fraction of the charge in the core,
bond and interstitial \rev{regions}; they sum to one.
\item[Non-radial gradient share] (\dn{zeta}) [0--1] $\zeta=1-\sum_k|\nabla\rho_k\cdot\hat{\mathbf u}_k|/\sum_k|\nabla\rho_k|$:
zero for spherical, non-overlapping atoms.
\item[Negative-Laplacian volume fraction] (\dn{lnf}) $N_v^{-1}\sum_k\mathbf 1[\nabla^2\rho_k<0]$: volume fraction where
charge is concentrated.
\item[Negative-Laplacian charge fraction] (\dn{lnf_charge_weighted}) $\sum_{\nabla^2\rho_k<0}\rho_k/\sum_k\rho_k$: the
same as a charge fraction.
\item[Valence Laplacian concentration] (\dn{lap_concentration_valence})
\rev{$\sum_{s_k>a,\,\nabla^2\rho_k<0}|\nabla^2\rho_k|/\sum_{s_k>a}|\nabla^2\rho_k|$}: the share of
the absolute Laplacian outside the cores where it is negative. Over the whole cell the same ratio is
$\tfrac12$ for every density, because $\int\nabla^2\rho\,\mathrm dV=0$ on a periodic grid.
\item[Mean bond ellipticity] (\dn{ellip_bond_bounded_avg}) [0--1) mean of $1-\lambda_2/\lambda_1=\varepsilon/(1+\varepsilon)$
over the \rev{bond-region} voxels with $\lambda_2<0$: zero for cylindrical charge.
\rev{With $b=\varepsilon/(1+\varepsilon)$, $\varepsilon=b/(1-b)$ recovers the usual ellipticity,
so $b$ orders the voxels as $\varepsilon$ does; its slope $1/(1+\varepsilon)^2$ is 1 at
$\varepsilon=0$ and 0.25 at $\varepsilon=1$, so only large values, where $\lambda_2\to0$, are
compressed. The descriptor is a voxel average over the bond region, not the ellipticity at the
bond critical points.}
\item[NCI volume fraction] (\dn{f_NCI}) volume fraction of the NCI region
$\{s_k<0.5,\ \rho_k<0.05$ a.u.$\}$, with
$s=|\nabla\rho|/\big(2(3\pi^2)^{1/3}\rho^{4/3}\big)$.
\item[Attractive NCI fraction] (\dn{NCI_attractive}) fraction of the NCI voxels with $\lambda_2<0$.
\item[Signed NCI density] (\dn{sign_lambda2_rho_mean}) [e\,bohr$^{-3}$] mean of
$\mathrm{sign}(\lambda_2)\rho$ over the NCI region.
\item[Site potential spread, interstitial potential minimum] (\dn{V_spread}, \dn{V_int_min}) [eV] standard deviation of the Hartree
potential energy $V(\mathbf G)=4\pi k_e\rho(\mathbf G)/|\mathbf G|^2$ (cell
average zero) over the atomic sites; its minimum in the interstitial \rev{region}.
\item[Mean bond-midpoint density, bond-midpoint density spread] (\dn{rho_mid_mean}, \dn{rho_mid_std}) [e\,\AA$^{-3}$] mean and standard
deviation of $\rho$, by Fourier interpolation, at the midpoints of the
first-shell bonds ($|\mathbf R_{ij}|\le1.1\,d_i$, $d_i$ the nearest-neighbour
distance of atom $i$).
\end{description}

\subsection{Structural (\rev{18})}
\begin{description}\sloppy
\item[Percolation densities] (\dn{rho_perc_a}, \dn{rho_perc_b}, \dn{rho_perc_c}) [e\,\AA$^{-3}$]
$\max\{c:\{\rho>c\}$ contains a cluster that winds around the cell along
$\mathbf a_\alpha\}$: density at the bottleneck of the best connecting path.
\item[Percolation anisotropy] (\dn{perc_anisotropy}) $(\max_\alpha-\min_\alpha)\rho_{\mathrm{perc},\alpha}/\mathrm{mean}_\alpha\rho_{\mathrm{perc},\alpha}$.
\item[Density maxima and minima per volume] (\dn{n_max}, \dn{n_min}) [\AA$^{-3}$] voxels higher (lower) than all 26
neighbours, per volume.
\item[Non-nuclear maxima per volume, non-nuclear-maximum charge] (\dn{n_NNM}, \dn{Q_NNM}) maxima \rev{outside every core sphere ($s_k>a$)},
per volume [\AA$^{-3}$]; charge in their steepest-ascent basins over
$Q_\mathrm{tot}$.
\item[Minimum density, relative minimum density, mean interstitial density] (\dn{rho_min}, \dn{rho_min_ratio}, \dn{rho_int_mean}) $\min_k\rho_k$
[e\,\AA$^{-3}$]; $\rho_{\min}/\langle\rho\rangle$; mean $\rho$ over the
interstitial \rev{region} [e\,\AA$^{-3}$].
\item[Gradient-direction eigenvalues] (\dn{T_eigenvalues_t1}, \dn{_t2}, \dn{_t3}) sorted eigenvalues of
$T_{ab}=\sum_k\partial_a\rho_k\partial_b\rho_k/\sum_k|\nabla\rho_k|^2$ (trace 1;
all $\tfrac13$ for a cubic crystal).
\item[Gradient fractional anisotropy] (\dn{charge_FA}) [0--1] $\sqrt{3/2}\,\lVert T-\tfrac13I\rVert_F/\lVert T\rVert_F$.
\item[Shannon entropy] (\dn{shannon_entropy}) $S=-\int\tilde\rho\ln\tilde\rho\,dV-\ln V$ with
\rev{$\tilde\rho=\rho_+/\int\rho_+\,dV$, where $\rho_+=\max(\rho,0)$, so that points with
$\rho\le0$ contribute nothing (Section~\ref{si:stability}, PAW pseudo-densities)}; zero for a uniform density.
\item[Disequilibrium] (\dn{disequilibrium}) $D=V\int\tilde\rho^{\,2}\,dV$; one for a uniform density.
\item[LMC complexity] (\dn{LMC_complexity}) $C=De^{S}$.
\end{description}

\subsection{Site heterogeneity (18)}
Each voxel is assigned to an atom with weight $w_i(k)$ (nearest atom by default;
\rev{Becke cells} optionally), and three per-atom quantities are
computed:
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
m_1^{(i)}=\frac{\sum_k w_i(k)\rho_k r_{ik}}{\sum_k w_i(k)\rho_k},\quad
f_\mathrm{bond}^{(i)}=\frac{\sum_{\rev{a<s_k\le b}}w_i(k)\rho_k}{\sum_k w_i(k)\rho_k},\\
\zeta^{(i)}=1-\frac{\sum_k w_i(k)|\nabla\rho_k\cdot\hat{\mathbf u}_{ik}|}{\sum_k w_i(k)|\nabla\rho_k|}.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
\rev{With the nearest-atom partition, $f_\mathrm{bond}^{(i)}$ gives each voxel to the
nucleus with the smallest $r_{ik}/R_i$ instead, so that the bond regions of the
atoms add up to that of the cell.} For each $X\in\{m_1,f_\mathrm{bond},\zeta\}$ the descriptors are
the site spread \dn{X_site_std} ($\mathrm{std}_iX^{(i)}$), the site range
\dn{X_site_range}, the site maximum and minimum \dn{X_site_max} and
\dn{X_site_min}, the within-element variance \dn{X_within_element_var}
($\sum_e x_e\mathrm{Var}_{i\in e}X^{(i)}$) and the between-element variance
\dn{X_between_element_var} ($\sum_e x_e(\bar X_e-\bar X)^2$), with $x_e$ the site fraction of element $e$;
the last two add up to $\mathrm{Var}_iX^{(i)}$. The within-element variance is \rev{zero}
(flagged) when no element has two or more atoms in the cell\rev{ (421 structures of
the dataset), the value every supercell of such a structure gives}, and the between-element
variance is zero (flagged) for an elemental crystal.

\section{Sensitivity and transfer per descriptor}
\label{si:stability}

This section gives, for every descriptor, its sensitivity to the numerical
choices (Section~3 of the main text) and its transfer to predicted densities
(Section~4 of the main text).

\rev{\paragraph{Absolute changes.} Relative changes mislead for values close to
zero, so Table~\ref{tab:si_stability} also gives the absolute change in units of
the standard deviation (SD) of each descriptor over the dataset. In the median structure, no
descriptor changes by more than 0.04\,SD in any test. With fourth-order
differences, all 54 descriptors change by less than 0.1\,SD in 95\% of the
structures and keep the order of the structures ($\rho_s\geq0.95$); with
second-order differences, 52 do so (not the bounded bond ellipticity,
0.15\,SD, and the second gradient-direction eigenvalue, $\rho_s=0.90$) and 46 on
the coarser grid. The eight sensitive to the grid are the counts of density
maxima, minima and non-nuclear maxima and the charge of the non-nuclear maxima,
because a coarser grid can miss a small extremum; the three within-element
variances, which are close to zero for most structures and keep their order
less well ($\rho_s=0.82$); and the site range of $f_\mathrm{bond}$ (0.11\,SD).
The fractional anisotropy changes by 20\% with second-order differences in
relative terms but by less than 0.05\,SD in 95\% of the structures.}

\paragraph{Atomic partition.} The whole-cell descriptors do not depend on the
partition. The 18 site descriptors do, by definition, because each partition
defines different per-atom regions (300 structures). Becke cells, which like
the nearest-atom partition ignore atomic size, preserve the ranking of the
structures by the site standard deviations \rev{of $m_1$ and $\zeta$ (Spearman
$\rho_s=0.99$ and 0.98). For $f_\mathrm{bond}$, whose per-atom regions under the
default partition follow the covalent radii (Section~\ref{si:defs}), $\rho_s$ is 0.74.}

\paragraph{Quantities pydemi does not offer.} Some standard quantities of
density analysis cannot be computed reliably from a \texttt{CHGCAR}. The
electron localization function and the kinetic, potential and total energy
densities need the kinetic-energy density, which a \texttt{CHGCAR} does not
contain; a gradient expansion of the kinetic energy (references in Section~1
of the main text) gives only an approximation, whose third-derivative terms
changed by 20--25\% with the derivative scheme or the grid. The voxel-averaged
bond ellipticity $\varepsilon=\lambda_1/\lambda_2-1$ diverges where
$\lambda_2\to0^-$\rev{;} pydemi uses the bounded form
$\varepsilon/(1+\varepsilon)=1-\lambda_2/\lambda_1$, which changes by \rev{0.23\%} on
the coarsened grid, 0.18\% with fourth-order and \rev{0.84\%} with second-order
differences. The numbers of saddle points of $\rho$ changed by 17--20\% on the
coarsened grid, and the grid census of critical points satisfied the Morse
relation of a periodic cell for only 9.1\% of the structures, so only maxima and
minima are counted. Magnetization densities are not used, because the predicted
densities of Section~4 of the main text have none.

\paragraph{PAW pseudo-densities.} A \texttt{CHGCAR} holds the pseudo-valence
density plus the compensation charge of the PAW method, which is pseudized
inside each augmentation sphere. The density is negative somewhere in 61\% of
the structures, so $\rho_{\min}$ mostly reflects the pseudization, and pseudized
atoms can lack a maximum at the nucleus \rev{and have one on a shell around it
instead. pydemi therefore counts as non-nuclear only the maxima outside every
core sphere: 11\% of the structures have such maxima, against 6\% when all
maxima inside the augmentation spheres are excluded, which would need the
augmentation radii, not part of a \texttt{CHGCAR}}. The interstitial density and the percolation levels, whose bottlenecks
lie near the sphere radius where pseudo and all-electron densities nearly
agree, are not affected. \rev{The Shannon entropy and the disequilibrium use only
the positive part of the density (Section~\ref{si:defs}). All other descriptors, including
the moments $m_n$ and the region fractions, use $\rho$ as it is, negative values included.
These values hold a median of $3.1\times10^{-4}$ of the valence charge and, in all but three
structures, lie within 0.5\,\AA{} of a nucleus.}

\paragraph{Density model.} The ChargE3Net architecture is used unchanged
(three interaction layers, $l_{\max}=4$, 4\,\AA{} cutoff; 1.9 million
parameters). The error of a predicted density is measured by the normalized
mean absolute percentage error,
$\mathrm{NMAPE}=100\%\times\sum_k|\rho_k^\mathrm{pred}-\rho_k^\mathrm{DFT}|/\sum_k|\rho_k^\mathrm{DFT}|$,
over the grid points $k$. The checkpoint shipped with pydemi (8\,MB) was
fine-tuned from the published Materials Project checkpoint on the densities of
the dataset (5,152 training, 302 validation and 605 test structures). The
Adam optimizer was used with a learning rate of $5\times10^{-4}$, multiplied by
$0.96^{s/3000}$ after $s$ steps, on batches of 8 structures with the density at
200 random grid points of each, and the mean absolute error of these values as
the loss. After each of the 78 passes over the training set (50,232 steps, about
18 hours on one NVIDIA RTX A5000), the NMAPE was computed on 400 grid points of
each validation structure, and the checkpoint with the lowest value (step
47,656) was kept. On 1,000 random grid points of each test structure, the
released weights used as they are give a median NMAPE of 32\%, because \rev{they
stand for the PAW potentials of the Materials Project, in which some elements
have a different number of valence electrons}; the same network trained from scratch on the dataset
(200,000 steps) gives 0.78\%, and the fine-tuned model 0.59\%, better than the
one from scratch for 470 of the 605 structures. The prediction grid is the
one VASP chooses for a 500\,eV cutoff with accurate precision, which reproduces
the grids of all calculations of the dataset. The predicted density is rescaled
to the valence electron count \rev{the structure has in the calculations of the
dataset}, and the model, the checkpoint hash and the scale factor are recorded
with the result. The copy of the network in pydemi reproduces the
predictions of the original ChargE3Net code to $6\times10^{-7}$ of the peak
density for the structures checked.

\paragraph{Accuracy of the predicted densities.} On the full DFT grids of the
605 test structures, the NMAPE of
the predictions is 0.72\% on average (median 0.58\%); crystals with lanthanides
or actinides are predicted less accurately, and the worst case, YbPrZn$_2$, has
10.4\%. Before rescaling, the predicted densities integrate to the nominal
electron count within 0.13\% (median; largest deviation 1.5\%). Over the \rev{54} descriptors the median
relative error is 0.66\%. Per structure, the median error over the \rev{42}
descriptors with $\rho_s\geq0.95$ is \rev{0.50\%} (90th percentile 1.2\%) and follows the
density error (Spearman \rev{0.46}; Fig.~\ref{fig:si_ml_structure}); it exceeds 5\%
only for Ba$_3$N, Li, TiH$_2$, Yb$_2$MgIn and YbPrZn$_2$.

\paragraph{The \rev{12} descriptors with $\rho_s<0.95$.} They fall into the four
groups of Section~4 of the main text. The counts of maxima and minima have
$\rho_s=0.77$ and 0.57 and median errors of 35\% and 38\%. The number and charge
of non-nuclear maxima have $\rho_s=\rev{0.73}$ and \rev{0.76} and are zero in both densities
for \rev{82\%} of the structures. The bounded bond ellipticity\rev{, the valence Laplacian
concentration} and the negative-Laplacian volume fraction, which use second derivatives, have median
errors of \rev{11\%, 10\%} and 6\%. The five anisotropy measures, namely the three
eigenvalues and the fractional anisotropy of the tensor of gradient directions
and the percolation anisotropy, have $R^2\geq0.987$ between the two densities,
and the median error of the eigenvalues is at most 0.07\%.

\section{Property models}
\label{si:props}

\paragraph{Targets.} All targets were downloaded from the Materials Project
(database version 2026.04.13) for the same material identifiers; the moduli are
the Voigt--Reuss--Hill averages. Elastic data were kept only where $K$ and
$G$ lie between 0 and 1000\,GPa and equal the values in the dataset of Kumar
et~al.\ (J.\ Chem.\ Inf.\ Model.\ 66, 7113, 2026) to 0.001\,GPa (6,000
materials), and $C_{11}$, $C_{12}$ and $C_{44}$ only where the stiffness tensor
is mechanically stable, with its smallest eigenvalue above 1\,GPa (5,791). The
direct-gap label is defined only for materials with a gap (1,836).

\paragraph{Comparison of Table~4.} For the comparison of Table~4 of the main text, LightGBM models
were also trained with the same protocol on two columns of the dataset of
Kumar et~al.: the
energy relative to the free atoms, and the Debye temperature of the 4,903
materials that have one there (equal to the Materials Project value for all of
them). For the same comparison we trained MatTen (Wen et~al., Digital
Discovery 3, 869, 2024) with its published code on the same split and target
values, one model with a scalar output per target, with the Adam optimizer
(learning rate 0.01, weight decay $10^{-5}$), batches of 32 structures, at most
300 epochs with early stopping on the validation error (patience 150), and the
checkpoint with the lowest validation error.

\paragraph{Training.} Inputs: the \rev{54} descriptors of pydemi. Ridge and logistic regression: median
imputation, standardization, penalty from $10^{-3}$ to $10^{3}$ (13 values)
chosen on the validation set. Random forests: 500 trees, $\frac13$ of the
features per split for regression and $\sqrt{n}$ for classification, median
imputation. LightGBM: missing values handled natively; 50 Optuna TPE trials
(sampler seed 0) over learning rate 0.01--0.1 (log), 7--127 leaves (log),
minimum leaf size 5--100 (log), feature fraction 0.3--1, bagging fraction
0.5--1 and $L_1$, $L_2$ penalties $10^{-8}$--10 (log); objective the squared
error of the (transformed) target or the log-loss; early stopping after 200
rounds without improvement on the validation set, at most 10,000 rounds.
$\log_{10}$ was applied to the refractive index. \rev{Four} of the 34 LightGBM models, those of \rev{the bulk modulus,} the cohesive energy, the Debye temperature and the Cahill conductivity with all descriptors, used more than 9,500 rounds on average, so a larger cap could improve them slightly. SHAP values are the
exact tree contributions of LightGBM on the test set, averaged over the seeds.

\paragraph{Ten descriptors per property.} For each property, the \rev{54} descriptors
were ranked by their mean absolute SHAP value in the five LightGBM models on the
\emph{training} set, averaged over the seeds; the ten highest
(Table~\ref{tab:si_top10}) were the only inputs of a second set of ridge, random
forest and LightGBM models, trained and tuned exactly as above
(Table~\ref{tab:si_full}).

\paragraph{Correlated descriptors.} On the 4,847 training structures, the
Spearman correlation $\rho_s$ was computed between all pairs of the \rev{54}
descriptors. Descriptors were clustered by complete linkage on $1-|\rho_s|$ with
a threshold of 0.10, so that every pair in a cluster has $|\rho_s|\geq0.90$;
in each of the 11 clusters with more than one member, the descriptor with the
largest mean SHAP share over the 17 properties (training set) was kept and the
other \rev{16} were dropped (Table~\ref{tab:si_redundant_groups}). The 17 properties
were retrained on the remaining \rev{38} descriptors exactly as above. The change in
test score and its 95\% interval come from a paired bootstrap of the test
materials (2,000 resamples) on the predictions averaged over the five seeds
(Table~\ref{tab:si_redundant_scores}).

\section{Automated tests}
\label{si:tests}

\rev{pydemi has 346} automated tests (Table~\ref{tab:si_tests}). They run on
Linux, macOS and Windows each time the code changes, and on our computer all of
them pass in 4 minutes. This section describes the tests that check the
descriptors themselves.

\paragraph{Tolerances.} A relative tolerance $\epsilon$ means
$|a-b|\le\epsilon\,\max(|a|,|b|)+10^{-12}$; for a field on the grid, the largest
difference is compared with the largest value. $\epsilon$ is $10^{-9}$ to
$10^{-12}$ where only round-off can make two results differ, $10^{-6}$ for the
invariance tests, whose cells are summed in a different order and transformed on
grids of different size, and $10^{-4}$ to a few per cent where a closed form for a
continuous density is compared with its value on a grid; these tests also check
that the error decreases when the grid is refined.

\paragraph{Reference densities.} Most library tests use densities whose exact
values are known: superpositions of normalized Gaussians,
$\rho(r)=N(\alpha/\pi)^{3/2}e^{-\alpha r^2}$, and of 1s Slater functions,
$\rho(r)=N\zeta^3\pi^{-1}e^{-2\zeta r}$, summed over periodic images and
evaluated on the grid together with their analytic gradient and Hessian
(\texttt{pydemi.validate.analytic}). The Gaussians are smooth and band-limited
on fine grids; the Slater functions have the nuclear cusp of a real density.
Most test cells are triclinic, so that terms that vanish for orthogonal axes are
exercised.

\paragraph{Invariance (\rev{163}).} A Fe$_2$O crystal in a triclinic cell
(three Gaussians on a $20\times21\times22$ grid) is processed as it is, as its
$2\times2\times2$ supercell, with its origin translated by $(3,7,11)$ voxels and
rotated by 37$^\circ$ about $(1,2,0.5)$. Each of the \rev{54} descriptors must agree
under each transformation to $10^{-6}$ (\rev{162} tests), and one more test checks
that the supercell has 8 times the atoms, volume and charge and that the
rotation preserves the metric. The tests are generated from the list of
descriptors, so a descriptor added later is tested too.

\paragraph{Derivatives (16).} The spectral gradient, Laplacian and
Hessian of a Gaussian superposition are exact to $10^{-10}$ in an orthorhombic
and a triclinic cell, and finite differences of order 2, 4 and 6 converge at
their nominal order. A Laplacian built from the diagonal of the metric alone is
exact for the orthorhombic cell but more than 5\% wrong for the triclinic one,
which is why the full metric is used. For the Slater density, with its cusp at
the nucleus, finite differences converge away from the nuclei, whereas the FFT
Laplacian rings across the cell and does not converge.

\paragraph{Geometry and partitions (\rev{16}).} Nearest-atom distances
agree with a brute-force search over $\pm5$ periodic images to
$10^{-12}$\,\AA{}, also in an extremely sheared cell in which a fixed
$3\times3\times3$ supercell misses the nearest image by more than 0.01\,\AA{}.
\rev{Both} partitions conserve the charge to $10^{-12}$.

\paragraph{Closed forms (40).} For a single Slater density
($\zeta=3$\,\AA$^{-1}$) the charge integrates to 1, $m_1=3/(2\zeta)$ holds to
$5\times10^{-4}$ and $m_2=3/\zeta^2$ to $1.5\times10^{-3}$, and the non-radial
gradient share is below $5\times10^{-5}$. Over the whole cell, the share of
$|\nabla^2\rho|$ where $\nabla^2\rho<0$ is $\tfrac12$ to $10^{-10}$. The
function $\cos2\pi x+\cos2\pi y+\cos2\pi z$ with a small periodic tilt has
exactly one maximum, one minimum and $3+3$ saddles per cell. A simple-cubic
lattice of Gaussians percolates exactly at the bond-midpoint density along all
three axes, with zero anisotropy. A uniform density gives zero Shannon entropy
and unit disequilibrium, and a layered density a fractional anisotropy of 1. The
Hartree potential of a Gaussian matches its closed form to $2\times10^{-3}$, the
within- and between-element variances add up to the total variance to
$10^{-12}$, and the bounded ellipticity equals the value from the exact Hessian
of an anisotropic Gaussian to $10^{-3}$.

\paragraph{Other tests.} The remaining tests check that the reader
gives the same density from every \texttt{CHGCAR} layout VASP writes, including
spin-polarized, non-collinear and VASP~4 files; that the density model
reproduces the prediction of the original ChargE3Net code to $10^{-5}$ of the
maximum density and is invariant to rotating the cell and permuting the atoms;
that the property models reproduce their stored test-set predictions to
$10^{-9}$; and that every figure of the graphical interface reproduces the value
of its descriptor.

\section{Dataset and cost of the run}
\label{si:dataset}

\paragraph{Structures.} The dataset of Kumar et~al.\ (J.\ Chem.\ Inf.\ Model.\ 66, 7113, 2026)
contains 6,059 VASP charge densities of 87 elements. Classes are assigned by the anions present,
first match: 86 elemental solids, 3,195 intermetallics, 354 borides and
carbides, 80 hydrides, 653 pnictides, 548 chalcogenides, 607 oxides and 536
halides. The cells contain 1--152 atoms (median 6) on grids of
$1.8\times10^5$ to $1.7\times10^6$ points (5th--95th percentile).

\paragraph{DFT calculations.} The structures were taken from the Materials
Project and relaxed with VASP, the PBE functional and PAW potentials, a
plane-wave cutoff of 500\,eV and a $k$-point spacing of 0.1\,\AA$^{-1}$: ionic
positions, cell shape and volume were optimized with the conjugate-gradient
algorithm until the residual forces were below $10^{-3}$\,eV\,\AA$^{-1}$, with
Gaussian smearing and an energy criterion of $10^{-5}$\,eV for the electronic
self-consistency. The density was then recomputed on the relaxed structure with
the tetrahedron method with Bl\"ochl corrections, which makes it independent of
a smearing width (Kumar et~al.). Each run folder holds the \texttt{CHGCAR} of
this last calculation; 4,901 also hold its \texttt{OUTCAR}, which records a
spin-polarized single-point calculation with one PAW dataset per element and an
energy criterion of $10^{-6}$\,eV. In 221 of these 4,901 runs (4.5\%, 133 of them
intermetallics) the self-consistency loop stopped at its limit of 200
iterations before reaching the energy criterion; these densities are kept. The
1,158 runs without an \texttt{OUTCAR} are assumed to follow the same protocol.

\paragraph{Cost.} The descriptors of all structures were computed in one
batch run without a failure, on our computer (two Intel Xeon Gold 6338N processors) with 16
worker processes; the run took 37 minutes. The time per structure has a median of
3.3\,s and a 95th percentile of 15\,s; added up over the structures, the run
took 8.4 CPU-hours. The time follows the number of grid points (Spearman
correlation 0.96) more closely than the number of atoms (0.77). The table
below gives the time and peak memory on one core for three files of different size.

\begin{center}

\begin{tabular}{lccc}

Predicting a density with ChargE3Net takes about 0.35\,ms per pair of an atom
and a grid point within the 4\,\AA{} cutoff on one CPU core; for the $40^3$ grid
of BeCo (2 million pairs) this is 12 minutes. A GPU makes it much faster.

\paragraph{PAW datasets.} The \texttt{OUTCAR} files of the 4,901 runs
that have one name one PAW dataset per element, the same for every structure of
the element. \rev{The name and valence charge of the dataset of every
calculation are deposited with the data.}
